# The Projection-Headroom Hypothesis (documented 2026-07-05)

Status: hypothesis, not a result. Generated by the necessity dissociation
(F(ii): raw class-mean +0.043 n.s., subspace-projected +0.341 at matched
norm) read against the diff-in-means steering lineage
(`novelty_check_newresults_2026-07-05.md`). Candidate centerpiece for the
next paper, AFTER the current paper ships and item G establishes recipe
transferability.

## Statement

Published diff-in-means steering methods (ALS 2509.18116, AdaRAS
2601.19847, Valentino 2505.12189, trajectory steering 2604.05655) deliver
their effect through a small causally-relevant component embedded in a
mostly-noise vector, and therefore pay two hidden taxes:

1. **Dilution tax** — at any fixed intervention norm, most of the budget
   is spent on causally-dead directions (SAE-RSV 2509.23799 measures raw
   diff-in-means vectors at ~93.6% noise features).
2. **Damage ceiling** — amplitude cannot be raised to compensate because
   the noise component harms the model first (our dose-response: x2/x4
   scaling turns destructive, −0.18/−0.16; rand_norm arms consistently
   negative).

Projecting the same vector onto a causally-identified low-rank subspace
removes both taxes. PREDICTION: on hard slices whose failures are
recoverable, causally-filtered versions of published steering vectors
outperform the published raw versions at matched norm — potentially by a
large factor where the current binding constraint is noise, not
recoverability.

## Evidence for

- F(ii): +0.043 → +0.341 from concentration alone (the same vector, same
  norm, same positions). F(ii)-b: the retained content is label-specific
  (shuffled −0.043, sign-flip −0.120) — the filter keeps signal, not
  generic energy.
- SAE-RSV: even non-causal (SAE-semantic) filtering improves steering
  success 45.6→56.4% — directional support from an independent group.
- Ali 2507.11771: CAA-style raw steering weakens with model size — the
  dilution account predicts exactly this (fixed effective component,
  growing ambient dimensionality); our 27B projected result runs against
  the trend, consistent with filtering restoring scale-robustness.

## Failure modes (what would falsify or bound it)

1. **Recoverability ceiling**: steering only fixes failures the model has
   the competence to avoid. Even our best arm captures ~half the oracle
   hint's effect (+0.40 vs +0.75). Where a benchmark's failures are
   capability gaps, no filtering helps. Prediction applies to hard slices
   with recognition-gap-like structure.
- 2. **Saturation**: ALS is at 91% on MATH-500 — no headroom exists there
   regardless. Test on their hard slices, not their headlines.
3. **Identification cost/transfer**: the causal subspace must be
   identifiable in the target setting (our pipeline: donor condition →
   delta capture → localization → rank compression → guards). Item G
   tests whether this recipe transfers at all (Qwen, our task). If G
   fails, the hypothesis is untestable cheaply and this doc's priority
   drops.
4. **Regime dependence**: prior raw vectors work at smaller scales /
   every-token application / other layers. The dissociation may be a
   large-model, sparse-position regime phenomenon. Either finding is
   publishable ("when do localized channels dominate?").

## The decisive experiment (next-paper centerpiece candidate)

Transplant the F(ii)/F(ii)-b design into a published setting (ALS's
MATH-500 / Qwen-2.5-7B is the cleanest: open recipe, big raw effect,
graded outcomes):

- Arms: (1) their raw class-mean (replication); (2) the same vector
  projected onto a causally-identified subspace at matched norm;
  (3) shuffled-label controls for BOTH; (4) sign-flip; (5) dose ladders
  for raw vs projected (the damage-ceiling prediction: projected
  tolerates higher amplitude before degradation).
- Causal identification in their setting via our pipeline (hint/oracle
  donor condition on math problems → delta PCA → LOO guards) — this is
  the real work, and is exactly the item-G recipe generalized.
- Readouts: hard-slice accuracy vs published; the raw-vs-projected gap;
  amplitude tolerance curves; label-specificity of their vectors (never
  tested by anyone).
