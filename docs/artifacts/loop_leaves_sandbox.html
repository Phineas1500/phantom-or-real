Phantom or Real · WikiHop port · September 2–3, 2026

The Loop Leaves the Sandbox

We could always read, from a model's hidden state, whether it was about to be right. The question was whether that signal is something the model uses or a coincidence it carries. On a synthetic puzzle task the answer was "uses." This is what happened when we tried the same trick on real reading-comprehension questions, then on a second model, then on two more tasks, then blind, where the passage contradicts what the model remembers, and finally on long documents full of tables.

Two tools, both made from the model's own insides

Take a question the model gets wrong even though the answer is in the documents in front of it. We give it a list of candidates, and it commits, eight times out of eight, to the wrong one. Two ingredients are involved.

The nudge (write)

A single vector added to the model's hidden state at layer 30, at the places where a candidate is mentioned in the text. Its meaning is roughly "attend to this one." It was built from other questions, by recording how a hint changes the state.

The meter (read)

A linear probe on a later layer's final state that predicts whether the model is about to be right. Fit once, on other questions, never touched again.

The loop

Nudge the model toward each candidate in turn, one branch per candidate. Pick the branch that looks best. Answer with it. No label is used anywhere: the nudge, the meter, and the choice all come from the model.

What happened, in thirteen findings

1 / 528gold repairs

The first port failed, and the failure taught us the direction. The nudge we started with, an average "correct minus incorrect" direction, did nothing at any layer or dose, even though the meter kept moving. It carries no information about which candidate you mean. The direction that does is the change a hint makes to the state, the one that built the sandbox loop in the first place.

27%of failures

Only one kind of failure can be fixed. About a quarter of the model's document-dependent errors flip if you simply tell it "pay attention to X." Those are the close-cousin cases: it answered "Paris" where the label was "1st arrondissement of Paris," "mammal" for "Paenungulata." The rest are firm beliefs in a competing answer, or debatable labels, and nothing moves them.

+0.30then +0.35, replicated

On those rows, the nudge works on real text. Writing the right candidate's vector fixes about a third of samples the model got wrong every time; writing a wrong candidate's vector fixes almost nothing. It replicated on a second, disjoint set of questions with every setting frozen.

+0.65with memory removed

One frozen vector, and it is strongest when the model cannot cheat. A single direction averaged over other questions works as well as each question's own. Rename every entity so the model cannot answer from memory (closed-book accuracy falls from 45% to 12%), and the same vector fixes 70% of samples, on two disjoint draws. What tells the model which candidate you mean is only where you write.

0.36vs 0.22, no probe

The best way to pick the branch is to watch what the model does. Three different probes on the hidden state capped out well below the oracle. The model's own outputs do better: pick the candidate whose nudge makes the model actually answer with that candidate. Registered on a fresh set of questions, that beats the original meter by 14 points and needs no probe at all.

+0.12 / +0.06blind: renamed / real

Run blind, it helps the failures and hurts the successes, and on real text the two cancel. Every earlier number sat on questions an oracle had picked. Without the filter, the loop raises accuracy across all document-dependent failures on both a renamed-entity frame and a real-text frame. But it also breaks about a tenth of the questions the model already had right. On renamed entities an abstaining version breaks nothing and keeps a third of the gain. On real text the failures outside the fixable quarter are firm, the abstaining version still breaks one row in sixty, and the frame nets about zero. The loop is a repair tool for questions already known to be wrong.

+0.37on a second model

The nudge works on a second model. The way of choosing does not. On Qwen3.5-27B, a different architecture, one frozen vector at the candidate's address repairs the model at Gemma's relative depth exactly as strongly as it repairs Gemma, confirmed on a third set of questions the vector had never seen. But watching what Qwen answers picks the right branch no better than chance: nudged toward a wrong candidate, Qwen goes along more readily than when nudged toward the right one. There, a probe on the hidden state closes the loop instead, at half the oracle. What transfers across models is the write. Which selector works is a property of the model.

×8attention at L32

The nudge lives in a band of the network, and it works by pointing attention. Run at every depth, the same frozen vector repairs only inside a band about a quarter of the network wide: layers 20 to 30 of Gemma's 62, layers 31 to 37 of Qwen's 64, with nothing above. Inside that band, a measurement of what it does: the position that produces the answer sends up to eight times more attention to the addressed span in the layers just above the write, and the amount it sends predicts whether the model adopts that candidate. And it is the direction that does this, not the disturbance: a random vector of the same size at the same spot, or the same vector with its sign reversed, repairs nothing at all and sends no attention to the span. "Attend to this one" is now a number, and it belongs to one specific direction.

+0.49HotpotQA, WikiHop's vector

It is not a WikiHop trick, and one vector serves three tasks. Two more reading tasks were put in the same frame: HotpotQA, with free-form multi-hop questions over ten paragraphs, and SQuAD, with one paragraph and short answers. Both show the same fixable quarter. On HotpotQA the nudge repairs about a third of samples and the loop picks correctly 23 percent of the time against 3 for the model alone, both registered in advance and both confirmed. The stranger part: the vector learned on WikiHop repairs HotpotQA and SQuAD better than vectors learned on those tasks themselves, and closes the loop on both. Along the way a bug in the grading job hid the question from the model for two runs; the model's own reply, asking for the missing question, gave it away, and the runs were redone.

+0.05whole frame, blind, twice replicated

Where the document contradicts memory, a groundedness check makes the loop work blind. We asked why the loop nets nothing when run blind on WikiHop and found the model answers those questions mostly from memory, and a firm wrong memory is what the nudge cannot move. So we built the regime where the passage contradicts what the model remembers, swapping the answer entity in Wikipedia paragraphs. A third of those failures are fixable. The loop as registered still did not help blind: the model's own answer always wins its own branch. But one label-free check, "is the answer actually a span of the passage?", flags the memory failures and only three percent of correct rows, and with it the loop lifts whole-frame accuracy by four to six points with at most one row harmed. That is the first blind gain outside the sandbox. It first appeared as a pre-named rider after the registered rule failed, so we registered it properly and ran it twice more: on a fresh draw of the same questions and on a second set built from SQuAD passages, with one frozen vector learned on WikiHop and nothing fit to the new data. It held both times, five and three points, with no correct answer broken. Its limit is honest: the check only fires when the model answers from outside the passage, which is exactly the situation where a retrieval system is being ignored.

+8points from one sentence

We tried to make the blind gain bigger, and learned that a sentence beats it. Four attempts, all registered in advance. A wider detector broke correct answers. A different layer or dose did not help: the recipe was already the best one for this regime. On a second model the effect appeared, five points, but with the vote we had relegated to a backup rather than the probe we had bet on, so the registered prediction failed. And the plainest baseline of all, telling the model in one sentence to follow the documents over its memory, raises whole-frame accuracy by five to eight points on its own, more than the loop, for free. Run on top of that sentence the loop adds about two more points, too few to call. The honest reading is that the nudge is a finding about mechanism, with a reach the sentence lacks: a fifth of the conflict failures are fixed only by attention, and a quarter of the failures the hint cannot touch still move under the write. As a product it is the smaller tool.

0.87best blind detector, real text

We tried the literature's detectors, and found a mirage and a better baseline. Two published ideas promised to spot the wrong answers the passage check misses: a probe on the model's hidden state at the answer's own tokens, and asking the model whether its answer is true. On the swapped-fact tests the probe looked perfect, and the loop it drove reached the ceiling of a perfect detector. Then we ran it on real WikiHop text, where nothing was swapped: it fell to 0.87, and the version trained on swapped facts transferred at chance. On those tests the right answer is always the out-of-place entity, and the probe had learned to see the seam. Asking the model to grade itself was at chance everywhere. One thing did land: context-aware decoding, a published trick that compares what the model says with and without the passage, picks the right candidate well enough to gain ten to eleven points on the conflict tests, more than the loop, the sentence, or reasoning, and nothing at all on WikiHop, where both candidates are in the passage. The paper's comparison table now has it at the top for the conflict regime.

−19points, tables, blind

Long documents full of tables were the last hope for a big blind gain, and the nudge does not survive them. We buried each answer's table among eight others, five thousand tokens of markdown. Here the model has nothing to remember, and 40 percent of its failures are the fixable kind, the most of any test; a text hint fixes nine in ten of them. But a keyword retriever meant to aim the nudge finds the right row for only a quarter of those, and the nudge itself misbehaves at this length: written across a row it wrecks the row and the model answers from somewhere else, losing 19 points blind and 42 when aimed at the right row; written only at the answer's own few tokens it either still harms, at the usual strength, or does nothing at a gentler one. The same nudge that fixed 55 to 75 percent of fixable failures on short passages fixes a tenth to a fifth here. The attention lapses are real and reachable by text. They are not reachable, blind or otherwise, by this nudge at this length.

What it means

The signal is real, not a phantom, on real text too. The model's computation responds to a write into the place a probe reads, and the model can recognize which of its own nudged states is the right one. Both halves of the sandbox loop survive, with a scope: the failure has to be one the model can be talked out of, and the write has to carry candidate identity through its address.

The write is the solved half. One vector, fit once, reused everywhere. It works best exactly where the sandbox worked, on novel names, which says it never depended on the model knowing the entities.

The selector is the honest limit. Reading the hidden state, even with a probe fit on the right data or on the nudged branches themselves, tops out around half of what the oracle achieves. Watching the model's answers does as well or better for free. The remaining gap is in what the model does with a nudge, not in how we read it.

The deployment reading landed, two frames

Run the loop blind on everything the model gets wrong because of the documents, and on questions it already gets right. Both readings confirmed their registered prediction: the failures improve, with intervals clear of zero. Both also failed the collateral bound set in advance: forced to answer, the loop breaks about a tenth of the questions the model already had right. The abstaining version, which only overrides the model when exactly one branch is accepted at least half the time, is collateral-free on renamed entities but breaks one row in sixty on real text. What separates the frames is reach. When entities are renamed and the model cannot answer from memory, the loop fixes some failures a hint could not. On real text those failures are firm. Net over a whole frame: about two points gained on renamed entities, about zero on real text, and ten or three points with a perfect detector of which questions are wrong.
