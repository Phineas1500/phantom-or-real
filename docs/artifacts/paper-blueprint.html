Gauge, Not Lever: Dissociating Readable Correctness from its Causal Repair Channel

In a model that visibly predicts its own failures, the readable correctness signal and the causal repair channel are different objects: the entire readable subspace can be deleted without behavioral cost, while repair flows through an identified low-rank channel that carries the label-specific content of natural success yet is itself not decodably privileged relative to random subspaces of its rank — gauge and lever, anatomically separated. And the ending the program earned this weekend: used in the one role its nature permits — as a judge over steered candidate futures rather than a lever — the gauge closes a self-repair loop into which no information about the problem being solved enters at test time (every learned ingredient was fit on other problems).

That paragraph is the paper's thesis, and here is what it means. Inside the model there is a signal that announces whether the upcoming answer will be right — like a fuel gauge on a dashboard. There is also, in a completely different place, the machinery that actually determines whether the answer comes out right — the steering wheel, or lever. Our finding: they are not the same thing. You can rip out the gauge entirely and the car drives exactly as before; and the lever that really steers the car turns out to be invisible to the instruments that read the gauge. ``Readable'' means a simple detector can find the signal; ``causal'' means changing it changes behavior. The whole paper is evidence that readable and causal come apart, in both directions. The story now has its ending, too: the gauge is useless to push on, but as a judge — scoring possible futures and picking one — it works as well as an answer key would — closing a repair loop into which no information about the problem being solved enters at test time (every learned part comes from other problems).

RefresherEight ideas everything else builds on

If you have been away from the project for a while, these are the terms the rest of the page leans on. Everything else is a combination of these.

The task (InAbHyD) We give the model invented mini-taxonomies (“every zumpus is a wumpus; wumpuses are mean…”) plus some observations, and ask it to propose the hypothesis that best explains them. Because the concepts are invented, the model can’t rely on memorized facts — it has to reason. “Strong correct” means it produced exactly the right hypothesis. Depth 1–4 controls how many steps of reasoning are needed.
Activations / residual stream As the model reads a prompt, each word position carries a long list of numbers (5,376 of them per layer in our model) — its working memory at that spot. All our experiments read or edit these numbers mid-computation.
Linear probe & AUC A probe is the simplest possible detector: a weighted sum of those numbers trained to predict something — here, “will the answer be right?” AUC scores the detector: 0.5 is a coin flip, 1.0 is perfect. Our probes reach 0.90+, i.e. the model visibly “knows” when it’s about to fail. “Readable” and “decodable” both mean: a probe can find it.
Directions, subspaces, rank Each list of 5,376 numbers is a point in a 5,376-dimensional space. A “direction” is one axis through that space; a “subspace” is a small bundle of axes; “rank 8” just means 8 axes. Big finding-shaped claims like “a rank-8 channel” mean: eight specific axes out of 5,376 carry the effect.
Steering, patching, erasing Three ways to intervene. Steering/patching: add or copy-in numbers at chosen positions to try to change the answer. Erasing/ablating: delete a signal (flatten those axes) and see if the model misses it. If deleting something changes nothing, the model wasn’t using it — that’s what “not necessary” means.
The class-mean vector Capture the model’s working memory on rows it naturally gets right, and on rows it naturally gets wrong; average each pile; subtract. The difference is a single direction that summarizes “what success looks like minus what failure looks like” — built purely by watching, no answers injected.
Controls (the placebo arms) Every positive result is paired with a version designed to work only if the boring explanation is true: random directions of the same size (is it just any nudge?), the same vector with shuffled labels (is it just any difference-shaped vector?), the vector flipped backwards (is direction meaningless?), random positions (does location matter?). A real effect passes when its placebos fail.
Pre-registration & confidence intervals Before running each experiment we committed, in writing, the decision rule for every possible outcome — so we can’t fish for a story afterwards. Numbers come with 95% confidence intervals in brackets; if the bracket contains zero, we treat the effect as not established. Nine times we also registered a directional bet in advance; all nine came true.

AbstractThe argument in eight steps

The gauge. Models linearly encode whether they will answer correctly (probe AUC up to 0.94, two models) — and the field just showed this readout resists steering. Plainly: a simple detector can see failure coming, but pushing on that signal doesn’t fix anything.
The gauge is not necessary. Deleting everything a probe can read — 9 axes × 5 layers × every word position — costs nothing (+0.05); deleting same-sized random axes is catastrophic (−0.38). Plainly: rip out the whole dashboard and the car drives fine; rip out random wiring of the same size and it dies.
The lever. Correctness is controlled where the ontology’s concepts are mentioned in the prompt: an 8-axis recipe written there repairs previously-failed problems (+0.25) and passes every placebo.
The necessity dissociation. The watch-and-average success vector does nothing applied whole (+0.04), but repairs strongly (+0.34) once squeezed into those 8 axes — and only with true labels (shuffled ≈ 0, flipped harmful).
The inversion. The 8 axes that causally control success are themselves unreadable — a probe looking only at them does no better than random axes. The mirror image of the gauge.
Answer-free repair. A version built with no per-problem answer information (its ingredients are fit on other problems’ graded runs) reaches +0.40 on problems where resampling the model 8 times yields nothing (majority vote: 0.000). Firing it on already-correct problems helps (+0.27).
The necessity coda (items K/K′). Deleting the model’s working memory content at the lever’s positions abolishes success entirely — but so does keeping ONLY the 8 repair axes. Plainly: the lever is a compact way to write; what it writes into is spread across the whole state.
The loop closes (L-series). List the candidate concepts from the prompt, fire the answer-free write at each in parallel, let the gauge score the results and pick one: +0.24 repair, matching oracle selection at our power — and no information about the problem being solved enters at test time (all learned parts come from other problems). Plainly: the dashboard can’t steer, but it can judge — and judging is enough to close a self-repair loop.

§5.2The gauge is not necessary

Fig 1 · item D pooled, 16 balanced rows · erase_raw continuity gate passed

The experiment: we found every direction from which any simple detector can read “correctness incoming” — nine of them, across five layers — and deleted them all at once, at every word position, for the whole computation. (The method, INLP, keeps retraining detectors and deleting what they find until nothing readable is left.) As the placebo, we did the identical deletion to random sets of nine directions. How to read the chart: each bar is the change in the chance of a correct answer; the whiskers are the uncertainty range; a bar whose whiskers cross the zero line means “no detectable effect.”
Figure 1 — erasure forest plot

Deleting the raw readout axis, then the entire readable stack: nothing. Deleting matched-rank random stacks with the identical estimator: collapse, with correctness-among-parsed falling to ~0.01 (content demolition, not formatting). Mechanism stated, not hidden: the readable stack carries 10–1300× less within-run variance than the random stacks — we claim non-necessity, never inertness. This directly discriminates the rival entanglement account, whose erasure hurt.

What happened: deleting the entire readable signal changed nothing (+0.047, and the bracket [−0.070, +0.203] straddles zero). Deleting random directions of the same size destroyed the model (−0.38) — so the deletion procedure itself has teeth; the readable signal is just not load-bearing. Jargon decoder: “variance telemetry” = we measured how much the deletion actually removes moment-to-moment, and the readable directions barely fluctuate — which is why deleting them is painless; “correctness-among-parsed” = even counting only outputs that still looked like answers, the random-deletion outputs were wrong — the damage is to thinking, not formatting; “the entanglement account” = a rival paper deleted their readable signal, hurt their model by a few points, and concluded the signal must be tangled into the computation. Our bigger deletion is harmless, so in our setting that explanation is ruled out.

§5.3–5.4The lever: localized, compact, specific

Figs 2–3 · guard v2 + specificity ladder, 26 fresh rows

Where the lever came from: when the model fails, it usually fails by never even proposing the right hypothesis — yet it recognizes the right answer instantly when offered it. Hinting the key concept fixes almost everything. So we compared the model’s working memory with and without the hint, at the exact word positions where that concept appears in the prompt (“concept-mention tokens”), and captured the difference (“hint-delta”). The “rank-8 recipe” is the 8 axes that best summarize those differences — always computed while holding out the problem being tested (“held out” / “LOO”), so the recipe never contains the test problem’s own answer.
Figure 3 — the specificity ladder

The rank-8 recipe repairs held-out failures; its average alone recovers a third; random subspaces matched in mean, norm, rank, and position do nothing; matched noise hurts. Paired (rank-8 − noise): +0.333 [+0.186,+0.489]. The specific directions carry the repair. (§5.3’s headline is the fresh-row concept patch +0.255; the dev-row +0.491 is reported as the labeled selected-row anchor.)

Reading the ladder: row 1 — writing the 8-axis recipe at the concept positions repairs failures (+0.245). Row 2 — writing just the average part of it recovers only a third of the effect, so the specific axes matter, not the average push. Row 3 — eight random axes with everything else identical (same size, same positions): nothing. Row 4 — random noise of the same magnitude: actively harmful. So the repair isn’t “any nudge of that size at those positions”; those particular eight directions carry it. Also worth knowing: potency is positional (the same write at random word positions does nothing), and the effect survives on fresh problems the recipe never saw — that’s the “fresh rows / guard” language, our insurance against having tuned to our own test set.

§5.5The F-series: what the channel carries

Fig 4 — the signature figure · 26 rows · all CIs pre-registered

The question here: the rank-8 recipe was built from hints — mild answer leakage. Can we drop the hints entirely? We built the class-mean vector by pure observation (average working-memory state on naturally-right problems minus naturally-wrong ones; see the refresher) and tried it five ways. “Full-dimensional” / “raw” = apply the whole vector as-is. “Into the channel” / “projected” = keep only the part of it lying along the 8 lever axes, same total strength. “Labels scrambled” = rebuild it after randomly relabeling which problems were right/wrong — same shape and size, meaningless content. “Sign-flip” = the true vector pointed backwards. “Donor-free” = direction from observation only and one fixed strength for every problem, so literally no per-problem answer information enters.
Figure 4 — necessity, labels, and answer-free repair in one ladder

One vector, five fates. Full-dimensional: null. Concentrated into the channel: +0.341. Labels scrambled (geometry, norm, positions identical): nothing. Direction flipped: actively harmful. Answer-free at test time (direction from watching other problems’ natural successes, amplitude from pooled scale): the strongest arm in the program. Baselines that no rival reports: majority-vote self-consistency scores 0.000 on these puzzles; even best-of-8 sampling caps at 0.192 — less than half the steered result. Deployment profile: collateral firing on correct puzzles is beneficial (+0.27); writing at all positions cancels (+0.03 n.s.) — addressing is the one remaining answer-adjacent ingredient. Item H closed it: pre-named answer-free position policies fail; the mechanism readout says the vector is a positional commitment command — content is the verb, the marked concept is the object.

Why this figure is the paper: the same vector at the same strength is useless spread over all 5,376 axes but strongly reparative squeezed into the right 8 — the content was there all along, diluted. The scrambled and flipped versions prove the meaning in the vector does the work, not its shape or size (both reviewers bet the scrambled one would work too; it didn’t). And the comparison that matters for “is this actually useful”: on these hardest problems, just re-asking the model 8 times and taking a majority vote scores zero — the model makes the same mistake every time — while the answer-free write fixes 40% of them. Jargon decoder: “self-consistency / best-of-8” = the standard sampling tricks; “collateral firing” = triggering the repair on a problem that was already right, which turns out to help rather than harm; “addressing” = the one thing still requiring answer knowledge is which concept’s positions to write at — write at all of them and the effects cancel out; “item H” = the experiment showing the vector acts like a “commit to the marked concept” command — it pulls the model toward whatever concept you point it at, right or wrong, which is why pointing matters.
And the removal side (the K/K′ coda): everything above shows the channel is sufficient — writing into it helps. Is it necessary — does natural success depend on it? Deleting those 8 axes at the concept positions abolished success on problems the model normally gets right (−0.67), while deleting 8 random axes cost almost nothing. But our own telemetry caught a catch: those 8 axes happen to hold ~98% of the sheer magnitude of the state there, so “deleting them” nearly empties the position. The follow-up matched that energy honestly: replace the state with a perfectly typical state (right size, generic content) — success still drops to literally zero; keep only the 8 repair axes and delete the other 5,368 — also zero. Conclusion, stated carefully: what the site truly requires is the state’s specific content as a whole. And a final registered control (K″, Aug 18) shows this necessity is anchored to the address: the identical destruction at random positions costs a third as much, and at other concepts’ mentions almost nothing — our 9th and last pre-registered bet, confirmed. The 8 axes are a compact write port, not a thin wire the whole computation runs through: write-compact, carry-broad.

The second model, decoded: we ported the whole playbook to a different 27-billion-parameter model (Qwen3.5). The same story holds — same failure mode, same kind of concept-position channel, same “only true labels work” texture — but every coordinate changes: the channel sits deeper in the network, needs ~16 axes instead of 8, and, most strikingly, the whole compression trick inverts: in Qwen the watch-and-average vector works as-is and squeezing it into the compact channel weakens it. Gemma concentrates its success-content into a few axes; Qwen spreads it across the whole state. Hence the summary sentence: the recipe is cross-model, the compression is not. One more twist: in Qwen the readable signal and the writable site split by position (readable at the last word of the prompt, writable at the concept mentions) rather than by direction — the same gauge/lever separation, drawn along a different axis of the architecture. Writing at Qwen’s readable site does nothing (its scrambled placebo matched exactly), which was our 6th pre-registered bet.

§5.5New — the addressing arc: the loop CLOSES

composition PASSED · replicated on a 2nd disjoint draw (+0.241 → +0.390) · frozen transfer +0.498 / +0.513

The paper's sharpest deflation was that the gauge is causally useless as a lever. The addressing arc ends with its rehabilitation, now behavioral and registered: in the corrected composition, the gauge scored ten steered candidate branches per row and its choices repaired +0.241 [+0.147, +0.342] — statistically indistinguishable from an all-knowing oracle's choices (−0.022, straddling zero), +0.259 better than matched-compute majority sampling, with wrong-address branches sitting exactly at baseline. No information about the problem being solved enters at test time: candidates come from the prompt, the write's content from watching natural successes, the selection from the gauge. And the rider settled the frozen-vector question the buggy run had faked: the July-frozen vector transfers to fresh rows at +0.498 — stronger than the in-job refit (+0.279).

Addressing arc · dP(strong), row-cluster bootstrap

The closed loop and its anchors. Delivery verified this time (targets-fired lift +0.180, CI>0 — the new tripwire). The fresh-draw replication landed: every result repeated on a disjoint 58-row frame, larger — the claim now stands on two draws.

Think of the model's internal fuel gauge again. You can't drive the car by yanking the needle — but shown ten possible steered futures, the gauge picks the genuinely-best one 19 times in 20. And the repair nudge works on brand-new problems if you mix it fresh from the model's own activity ("in-job, leave-one-out" = fitted on the spot, never using the row being repaired). Then came the night's real lesson. The composition run — gauge picks the branch, fresh-mixed nudge fires — came back all-null, and for a few hours the story looked like "the write is protocol-fragile." The truth was better and worse: a bug audit showed the branch generations never had the nudge plugged in at all — the code steered the measurement pass but not the answer-writing pass. (The giveaway: steered writes leave a fingerprint — they pull answers toward the marked concept — and these outputs had no fingerprint.) So the "the frozen vector does nothing" result from two days ago is also void: that test simply never ran. Everything was fixed, re-registered, and relaunched the same night — including a new automatic tripwire that voids any future run whose outputs lack the steering fingerprint, and a rider that finally runs the frozen-vector test for real. The original question — can the loop close answer-free? — got its answer when the corrected run landed: yes. The gauge's picks repair as well as a referee-with-the-answer-key's picks would, the fresh-mixed nudge works, and — the rider's bonus — even the year-old-style frozen vector from the July fridge works on brand-new problems, better than the fresh-mixed one (+0.498 vs +0.279). And the replication on a fully new set of problems came back even stronger (+0.39, with the loop beating every sampling baseline outright) — the paper now calls it settled: two draws, all gates green.

SJNew — can the model say what its gauge knows? Two censuses

registration-free reconnaissance · givemeanode H100 · ≈$2.50 total · Aug 14

We replayed 2,000 of the model's own stage-1 answers and asked it, verbatim: "Was your final proposed hypothesis exactly correct? yes or no." It says "no" to 85% of the answers it actually got right (8.2% yes overall, 97.5% unanimity across samples). Drop the word "exactly" and the picture inverts: 50.2% yes, discriminating 68% yes on correct vs 32% on wrong. One word flips verbal self-assessment from degenerate self-denial to a roughly calibrated signal — while the internal gauge reads AUC 0.94 under either phrasing.

The gauge is readable by a probe — but can the model read it out loud? Barely, and only if you ask exactly right. Asked "were you exactly correct?", the model reflexively says no to almost everything, including its successes — like a student who answers "probably wrong" to every "are you sure?" regardless. Soften the question and real self-knowledge appears (it says yes twice as often when it actually was right). Two lessons: the verbal channel is extremely fragile to phrasing while the internal signal is not — so the probe is not just reading the model's inner speech; and any experiment that uses the model's own verbal self-judgement must pin the exact question wording in advance (ours will cite both censuses, no cherry-picking). Update, Aug 18 — the causal version landed (item M): on a balanced grid of (actually right/wrong × says right/wrong), the gauge sides with what's actually true on 84% of the disagreement rows — it reads reality, not the model's self-story — and the repair nudge fixes problems the model swears it already got right exactly as well as ones it admits failing (+0.62 vs +0.50; our 8th pre-registered bet, confirmed). Both the gauge and the lever ignore the model's self-report entirely.

Q3.8New — day one with Qwen3.8-27B: the gap and the gauge in a hybrid

released the morning of Aug 14 · smoke + gauge hunt ≈$6 · reconnaissance, NOT in the paper

Qwen3.8-27B is a hybrid architecture: only every 4th layer is ordinary attention; the other 48 of 64 layers use DeltaNet-style linear attention. Within hours of release we had it running our task. The recognition gap is there at full strength (unhinted 0.309 at n=2,000; hinted 0.834; the hint repairs 81% of failing rows). The gauge is there too — a probe ladder over all 65 depths shows the familiar profile, rising smoothly to a peak at layers 51–54 (~82% depth) — and it reads identically from attention-layer and DeltaNet-layer residual streams (0.753 vs 0.752). The strength question resolved the same evening: the first-pass peak of 0.741 was label noise (our quick labels came from single temperature-0.7 samples, which agree with deterministic greedy answers on only 78% of rows — a state cannot predict sampling luck); against greedy labels, matching the other models' protocol, the peak is AUC 0.860 — near Gemma's 0.90, with less probe-training data than either registered gauge. No architecture-sensitivity claim survives.

Why this matters: every gauge we have measured so far lived in a model whose every layer can look back at the whole prompt (ordinary attention). Qwen3.8 mostly can't — three quarters of its layers carry a compressed running summary instead (that is what "linear attention / DeltaNet" means: cheaper, but no going back to re-read). If the know-your-own-failure signal were a product of full attention, it should weaken or move here. The verdict: same failure mode, same hint response, same signal at the same relative depth at essentially full strength, spread evenly across both layer types. Along the way we caught our own confound — sloppy quick labels made the gauge look weaker for a few hours, and fixing the label protocol recovered it — itself a portable methods lesson: compare gauges only under matched labels. This model is now the natural subject for the next paper's bridge experiments, both entry gates pre-cleared, and it cost about $6 to find out on day one.

§6Discussion commitments

Information location ≠ causal control, in both directions — with consequences for probe-based monitoring and a testable prediction for the steering lineage (the projection-headroom hypothesis: published raw-vector methods pay a dilution tax and a damage ceiling that causal filtering removes). Honesty ledger: endogeneity tested and null at this layer/rank; misdirection asymmetry named as an open anomaly; "an identified sufficient channel," not the unique mechanism; MDE statements on every null; brittleness and entropy telemetry listed as unrun. New (K/K′): necessity at the lever's site is real and content-specific but not compact — the write-compact / carry-broad asymmetry replaces the deferred "channel-in-use" upgrade; the anatomical mirror of item D is stated with the energy caveat. New (L/L′/censuses): §6 now carries "the gauge is not the verbal channel — and not useless": deletable-without-cost and degenerate in the model's own mouth, yet a state-level selector over candidate branches (gold address by score, 54/57); the repair recipe replicates on fresh draws when re-fitted in-job; and §5.5 discloses the caught execution defect — the earlier "frozen write fails / protocol transfer is the gap" reading is withdrawn as void; the corrected composition then PASSED (+0.241, gauge==oracle) and the frozen write transfers (+0.498) — the loop-closure sentence enters §6 with the replication caveat.

Decoding the commitments: “probe-based monitoring” — if you deploy a probe to watch for failures, our result says that can work (the gauge is real) even though the probe reads nothing the model uses — so don’t assume a good monitor gives you a control knob. “Projection-headroom hypothesis” — a prediction for the steering literature: published methods that add raw difference vectors are paying a dilution tax (most of the vector is inert filler) and should improve if filtered through a causally-identified channel — in Gemma filtering was required; in Qwen, where content is spread out, raw works and filtering hurts. “Endogeneity tested and null” — we checked whether the channel is the model’s own internal “decision variable” and could not show that; we claim only that our vector carries content about success and works when written in — “exogenous mediation,” an outside handle that works, not a discovered inner monologue. “Misdirection asymmetry” — the odd fact that our writes can push the model toward right answers but not steer it toward specific wrong ones; still unexplained. “MDE” — minimum detectable effect: for every “no effect” claim we state how big an effect our sample could have caught, so a null means “smaller than X,” never “exactly zero.”
